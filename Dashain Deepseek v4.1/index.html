The story

Four small scenes, one old meaning

Dashain is ten days long and a lifetime deep. Here is the short version. There are lamps to light, seeds to water, a kite to fly and a tika to receive — none of it is only for reading.

Scene One · Nine Nights

The night that had to end

Dashain opens with Ghatasthapana: a pot of seeds is set down in a dark room, and for nine nights Durga is said to fight the buffalo-demon Mahishasura. Every one of those nights looks as if it might be the last.

On the tenth morning, she wins. Vijaya Dashami is not really a story about power. It is the stubborn, ordinary fact that darkness never gets the final word.

Good does not win because it is stronger. It wins because it keeps coming back.

0 of 9 nights lit.

Scene Two · Jamara

What grows in the dark

The seeds sown on the first day are kept away from sunlight. They come out pale gold, thin and tender — jamara, grown for ten days without ever seeing the sun.

On Dashami, an elder tucks a few blades behind your ear. You are being handed something grown in the dark and given in the light: patience, turned into a blessing.

Some things can only grow where no one is watching.

Seeds in the dark. Nothing to see yet.

Scene Three · Tika

The blessing is not earned

Rice, yogurt, vermilion. An elder presses tika to your forehead and, with it, the same gesture their grandmother made — seven generations of one hand over one forehead.

Jamara goes behind your ear. Dakshina is folded into your palm. You did nothing to qualify for any of it, and that is exactly the point.

Tika is inheritance, not reward. It is given because you came home.

May you live long, may your heart stay soft, and may you always find your way back to this doorstep.

Scene Four · Kites & Ping

The sky remembers how to play

On Dashami the rooftops fill with kites, and the lanes fill with bamboo swings. A turn on the ping lasts three seconds; the laughing about it lasts the whole year.

Trains, buses and motorbikes all point the same way — home. The festival's real currency is a doorway that still knows your name.

Joy is its own kind of gratitude.

Both of them are waiting.

The last scene · your wish

Happy Dashain, dear one

May the lamps you lit tonight keep burning in ordinary places — in your kitchen, in your work, in the quiet after a hard day.

May your home stay loud with laughter.
May your hands find good work.
May every door you knock on open.
