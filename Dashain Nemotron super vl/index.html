The Victory of Good Over Evil

Long ago, the demon Mahishasura spread fear across heaven and earth. The gods combined their power and created Goddess Durga — radiant, fearless, riding a lion. For nine nights they battled, and on the tenth day — Vijaya Dashami — good triumphed over evil. Dashain celebrates the light within us conquering the darkness.

Jamara — The Seed of New Life

Before the festival, families sow maize and barley seeds in a dark corner of the home. By the tenth day they sprout into golden grass called jamara. A living promise: from small seeds, with care and time, grows prosperity and new beginnings.

Tika — The Elder's Blessing

On Vijaya Dashami, elders mix rice, yogurt, and vermilion into tika, place it on the foreheads of their loved ones, and tuck jamara behind their ears. It is more than a mark — it is a blessing of long life, strength, protection, and love passed from generation to generation.

Kites, Swings & Togetherness

Skies fill with kites, bamboo swings (ping) creak with laughter, homes wear new clothes, and families gather for feasts. Old disagreements are forgiven, doors open to guests, and the whole country celebrates as one big family.

A Light That Stays With You

Like a diya that keeps burning through the night, may the spirit of Dashain stay with you — Durga's courage, the harvest's joy, and the warmth of blessings from those who love you.
